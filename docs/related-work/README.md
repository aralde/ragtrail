# Ragtrail occupies a gap that neighbours almost fill

No work found in this review caches what ragtrail caches: the cross-question **retrieval path** of an agentic RAG run (searches, opened ids, cited ids, never the answer), checked per node by content hash at read time, with partial downgrade. That said, nearly every ingredient has a published precedent. **Agentic Plan Caching (APC, NeurIPS 2025)** caches plan templates from successful agent runs, evaluates on QASPER, and reports −50% cost at ~97% of no-cache accuracy. **Proximity (Middleware '25)** caches top-k document ids for similar queries in SimHash LSH buckets. **Alici et al. (SIGIR 2011)** validate cached search results at hit time against document timestamps. **GroundedCache (2026 preprint)** gates RAG answer reuse on source-version validity. Agent-memory work (Synapse, AWM, ReasoningBank, QCR) shows reused trajectories raise success rates when they are filtered for success. The same literature shows they lower accuracy when they are not: unfiltered self-generated memories fall *below* a no-new-memory baseline by 4–12 points ([Xiong et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16067v2)). That matters most for ragtrail's evaluation. Its +11 to +16 point accuracy gains come from ORACLE warm-up trails over 100% paraphrased repeats, judged by a same-family model validated on 40 labels. Prior art treats that setup as an upper bound on per-hit benefit, not a system result. The defensible claim today is a novel *combination* plus a novel measurement: fixed query context inflating lexical similarity to 97% firing. No individual mechanism is novel. The coverage here rests on web search and abstracts through October 2026. MERMAIDS (IEEE CISCE 2026) was read in full afterwards (see below): it does reuse evidence across questions in agentic RAG, so that idea alone is not new; ragtrail differs in storing hash-validated pointers instead of copied evidence. APEx, MIA and Hierarchical Caching were seen only at snippet level and must be read before claiming priority.

## Agent caches split into cost-saving plan caches and accuracy-raising memories

The agent-reuse literature falls into two camps with opposite goals, and ragtrail's results straddle them awkwardly. **Serving-oriented caches** reuse plans or tool results to cut cost and aim for accuracy parity. **Experience memories** reuse trajectories or distilled strategies as in-context guidance to *raise* success. None of the confirmed items in either camp targets the retrieval path of a RAG agent, and almost none handle staleness against a changing corpus.

APC is the closest serving-side neighbour. It extracts **plan templates** from *successful* runs using rules plus a small LLM. It matches new tasks by **exact match on an LLM-extracted intent keyword** and adapts the template with LLaMA-3.1-8B. Its benchmarks include FinanceBench, **QASPER** and GAIA via Open Deep Research. Results: **−50.31% cost, −27.28% latency, 96.61% of optimal accuracy**. On GAIA, cost falls from $69.02 to $16.27 while accuracy moves 37.58% → 36.97%. Its own ablation shows that loosening matching hurts: fuzzy matching at >0.6 similarity raises hits from 46% to 64% but drops FinanceBench accuracy from 85.5% to 77.0%. It has no invalidation beyond LRU and auto-disable on low hit rate ([APC](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.14852v2)). APC caches *how* to proceed. Ragtrail caches *where the evidence was*, so APC is the natural cost/accuracy baseline on a shared benchmark.

Other serving caches cover adjacent ground. **AgentReuse** extracts key parameters and an intent class before similarity matching. It reports 93% effective reuse and −93% latency, with no staleness handling ([arXiv 2512.21309](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.21309)). **TRIAGE** (preprint, Sep 2026) routes identical queries to prior results and similar queries to parameter-substituted "trajectory-as-a-skill" replays. It reports 62–76% token savings; accuracy was not visible on the abstract ([arXiv 2609.01428](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01428)). **Hierarchical Caching for Agentic Workflows** (MDPI MAKE 2026) caches at workflow and tool level with dependency-graph invalidation; its numbers were not retrievable ([MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2504-4990/8/2/30)). **TVCache** guarantees state-identical tool-output hits through longest-prefix matching on tool histories ([arXiv 2602.10986](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2602.10986)). **AgentRR** records and replays experiences with a "check function" as trust anchor, but reports no quantitative results ([arXiv 2505.17716](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17716)).

On the memory side, accuracy gains are the norm.

- **Synapse** (ICLR 2024) retrieves whole successful trajectories as exemplars. It reports 99.2% on MiniWoB++ and +56% relative step SR on Mind2Web ([ICLR](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/52f050499cf82fa8efb588e263f6f3a7-Abstract-Conference.html)).
- **AWM** (ICML 2025) induces abstracted workflows, with +24.6% relative step SR on Mind2Web and +51.1% relative SR on WebArena ([PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/wang25bx.html)).
- **ReasoningBank** (ICLR 2026) stores distilled strategies from self-judged successes *and* failures, retrieved by cosine top-1. It reports +4.6 to +8.3 WebArena points and beats Synapse and AWM ([arXiv 2509.25140](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.25140v2)).
- **ExpeL** (AAAI 2024) is the only classic memory paper with a retrieval-QA benchmark. On HotpotQA, distilled *insights* helped more than raw trajectories ([arXiv 2308.10144](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.10144)).

The most conceptually important neighbour is **QCR** (preprint, Aug 2026). It shows that *full-trajectory injection degrades* when entities, constraints or source-specific values change. Storing a procedure plus bindings, applicability conditions and verification requirements gives **+10.7 points over full-trajectory injection with 48.9% fewer tokens** on 2,391 WebArena/WorkArena/AppWorld targets ([arXiv 2608.12847](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12847)). That supports ragtrail's choice to return ids or *current* text rather than stale content.

## Retrieval caches reuse ids, but none follows a multi-step path

Retrieval-level caching is ragtrail's mechanistic home, and two properties are not new: caching *ids instead of text or answers*, and LSH bucket lookup. **Proximity** keys a cache on the query embedding and stores the vector DB's **top-k document indices**. Its LSH variant uses Charikar random-hyperplane signatures, 8 bits by default, with 20-entry buckets. Results: **−77.2% DB calls and −72.5% retrieval latency** on skewed MedRAG with 4.8 µs lookups, and near-full recall at small thresholds that degrades at large ones. It caches a single-shot NN result and **does not discuss corpus updates or invalidation** ([Proximity](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.05530v3)). Ragtrail's embedding SimHash band buckets are therefore close prior art for the optional semantic path. Its lexical MinHash/LSH path descends from similarity caching theory ([Chierichetti et al., PODS 2009](https://bibtex.github.io/PODS-2009-ChierichettiKV.html); [Pandey et al., WWW 2009](https://ir.webis.de/anthology/2009.wwwconf_conference-2009.45/)).

Newer vector caches handle freshness better. **Aker** (PVLDB 2026) uses per-entry adaptive thresholds and a "del-consistency" model: deletions apply eagerly, insertions lazily, with bounded-staleness refresh. It reports up to +64 pp recall over prior approximate caches ([arXiv 2609.03712](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03712)). **ARC** is the only cache paper framed for "Agent RAG". It keeps a per-agent passage cache scored by distance-rank frequency and hubness, at 0.015% of the corpus with up to 79.8% has-answer rate and −80% retrieval latency. It is a passage cache, not a per-question path ([arXiv 2511.02919](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.02919)). Intra-episode reuse also exists (DeepPlanner caches search results within one run; [arXiv 2510.12979](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.12979)), as does a large, orthogonal KV-state family that speeds prefill on retrieved chunks (RAGCache, CacheBlend, Cache-Craft; [RAGCache](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.12457), [Cache-Craft](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15734)). That family stacks with ragtrail rather than competing with it.

Ragtrail's invalidation has its clearest ancestors in classic web-search result caching. **Blanco et al. (SIGIR 2010)** use cache invalidation predictors to invalidate only the entries affected by index updates, with up to 30% fewer unnecessary re-evaluations than TTL ([SIGIR'10](https://www.dc.fi.udc.es/~roi/publications/sigir2010b.pdf)). **Alici et al. (SIGIR 2011)** compare a cached entry's generation timestamp with its documents' and posting lists' timestamps *at hit time*. Any change in ids or order makes the whole entry stale ([METU PDF](https://open.metu.edu.tr/bitstream/handle/11511/48372/index.pdf)). **Cambazoglu et al. (WWW 2010)** refresh stale entries proactively instead of just evicting them ([WWW'10](https://www.site.uottawa.ca/~nelkadri/CSI5389/Papers/27-Cambazoglu_et_al_WWW2010.pdf)). Ragtrail's per-node hash check is a content-addressed, per-item form of Alici's timestamp check. The **partial downgrade** (drop changed nodes, keep the rest, mark stale only when all changed) was not found in any retrieval cache, where results are treated as atomic.

The same classic work names a blind spot ragtrail shares. Hash validation detects *changed* cited nodes but not *new* documents that would now be better evidence. Blanco and Aker address insertions explicitly; ragtrail relies only on TTL for this "missing new evidence" case. The conceptual lineage runs back further: **Raghavan & Sever (SIGIR 1995)** proposed reusing "past optimal queries" and presenting their results ([IR Anthology](https://ir.webis.de/anthology/1995.sigirconf_conference-95.41)). **Fitzpatrick & Dent (SIGIR 1997)** used documents from similar past queries as feedback ([IR Anthology](https://ir.webis.de/anthology/1997.sigirconf_conference-97.34)). **Teevan et al. (SIGIR 2007)** found that up to 40% of queries are re-finding queries, often reworded but leading to the same results ([SIGIR'07](https://people.csail.mit.edu/teevan/work/publications/papers/sigir07.pdf)).

## Answer caches teach ragtrail about false hits, scoping and attacks

Semantic answer caches are the opposite design choice: they serve the stored answer. Their safety literature still transfers almost directly. **vCache (ICLR 2026)** shows that correct and incorrect hits have nearly identical similarity distributions (mean 0.84 vs 0.85). Static thresholds of 0.97–0.99 still give 2.5–5.2% error, and error grows as traffic accumulates. Its fix is a per-entry learned threshold with a user-set error bound ([vCache](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.03771v5)).

**GroundedCache** (preprint, May 2026) is the closest RAG analogue to ragtrail's read-time validation. It serves a cached answer only if four gates pass: query similarity, evidence overlap, **source-version validity**, and lexical support from *freshly retrieved* evidence. Naive caching gives 15–35% unsafe-served rates on HotpotQA and 51.5% under mtRAG document drift; GroundedCache brings these to 0.0% and 1.5% ([GroundedCache](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27494)). **FinCacheServe** ties reuse to evidence fingerprints and filing versions, seen at abstract level only ([arXiv 2607.26076](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.26076)).

Because ragtrail always regenerates the answer from current content, a false trail hit degrades retrieval rather than serving a confidently wrong answer. That is a genuine structural safety advantage, but it is not immunity, as the memory section below shows.

On isolation, the literature separates **confidentiality** leaks from **integrity** attacks. Confidentiality leaks come through timing: the ICML 2025 audit found global cross-user prompt-cache sharing at 7 of 17 API providers ([Gu et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/gu25b.html)). Integrity attacks come through planted collisions. **CacheAttack** reaches 80–88% hit rates against AWS Bedrock and Azure APIM semantic caches and cuts agent tool-selection accuracy by up to 84.5%. Per-user isolation blocks it, while key salting only partly helps ([arXiv 2601.23088](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.23088v2)). **KVGov** separates KV-cache keys per principal with HMAC salts ([arXiv 2608.09225](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09225)). Vendors expose exact-match partitions: Redis LangCache attributes, Portkey namespaces, Helicone cache seeds ([LangCache](https://redis.io/docs/latest/develop/ai/context-engine/langcache/concepts/); [Portkey](https://portkey.ai/docs/product/ai-gateway/cache-simple-and-semantic)). No surveyed work scopes caches by **permission group** rather than user or tenant. A shared trail store also remains a poisoning surface ([AgentPoison](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.12784)).

Ragtrail's **fixed-context finding** (97% firing, ~70% wrong until title or product name moved into the scope) has convergent but weaker precedent. The best evidence is a GitHub experiment in which an entity guard served **453 wrong answers per 1,000** product/country questions, residually failing on "a different question about the same product" ([semcache](https://github.com/Broikos-Nikos/semcache)). Practitioners recommend hashing context exactly and comparing only the variable part ([TrueFoundry](https://www.truefoundry.com/blog/semantic-caching-llm-gateway)). The NDSS 2026 "system prompt collision" attack exploits the same effect adversarially ([NDSS 2026](https://www.ndss-symposium.org/ndss-paper/cache-me-catch-you-cache-related-security-threats-in-llm-serving-frameworks/)). No peer-reviewed paper isolates this effect quantitatively, so ragtrail's measurement is a publishable contribution in its own right.

## Search-agent memories reuse how to search, not what was found

Memory work for deep-research agents stores strategy, not evidence. **LRAT** mines agent trajectories offline, treating browsed documents as positives and unbrowsed ones as negatives, to train a dense retriever. It reports 16.8–37.9% evidence-recall gains on BrowseComp-Plus and up to ~30% fewer steps, but replays nothing question-specific at inference and ignores citations ([LRAT](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.04949v1)). **ITER** adds agent context to the retriever and finds that "visited-and-useful" documents work best as redundancy negatives ([arXiv 2608.27912](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27912)).

Inference-time memories store compressed search trajectories or workflows:

- **MIA** ([arXiv 2604.04503](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.04503))
- **APEx** ([arXiv 2609.02253](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.02253))
- **Memento** ([arXiv 2508.16153](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.16153))
- **R²-Mem** ([arXiv 2605.13486](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13486))

**Databricks MemAlign** reports reasoning steps dropping from ~20 to ~5 on Genie text-to-SQL spaces, with no variance reported ([Databricks](https://www.databricks.com/blog/memory-scaling-ai-agents)). **MERMAIDS** (Huang et al., IEEE CISCE 2026, pp. 1587–1592, [DOI 10.1109/CISCE69494.2026.11504621](https://doi.org/10.1109/cisce69494.2026.11504621); preprint [Research Square 10.21203/rs.3.rs-9455786/v1](https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-9455786/v1), read in full) is the closest overlap found. It adds a Cross-task Evidence Reuse Cache (ERC) to agentic RAG: it stores *verified claim–evidence–source–time tuples* (extracted text, never final answers, "to prevent leakage") from previously completed queries, matches each *sub-query* by FAISS cosine ≥ 0.85, and on a hit reuses the cached evidence directly instead of retrieving. Freshness is TTL only (24 h on MultiHop-RAG, 7 days on Wikipedia sets); there is no content or version check. With GPT-4o-mini on HotpotQA, removing the ERC costs 1.5 EM points and raises average retrieval calls from 3.8 to 5.6; queries run in official dataset order, with no controlled repeat workload. Its own error analysis attributes **19% of 100 sampled HotpotQA errors to cache staleness** ("reused evidence is outdated"). Overlap with ragtrail: cross-question evidence reuse in an agentic RAG loop, never caching answers, verified-only admission, TTL. Differences: ragtrail stores node *pointers* with content hashes and re-reads current text (prefetch) or hints ids, validated per node at read time — aimed at exactly the staleness failure MERMAIDS reports; ragtrail matches the whole question's path, not sub-queries; key-value LSH instead of a vector index; permission-group scoping; and a controlled evaluation (paraphrased repeats, oracle vs self-recorded trails, must-not-match controls).

Within-episode evidence also tempers expectations for prefetch mode. **AgentIR** found that carrying forward prior *queries* and reasoning helps more than carrying forward retrieved *documents*, because irrelevant documents propagate errors ([arXiv 2603.04384](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.04384v3)). Real traffic supports the premise of repetition: a 14M-request log study found fact-seeking agent sessions repeat more over time and recommended early stopping on repeated queries ([Agentic Search in the Wild](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17617v2)).

### Reused memories demonstrably hurt accuracy

The strongest controlled evidence comes from **Xiong et al.** Agents copy retrieved records when inputs are similar ("experience-following"), and flawed records propagate. Adding all self-generated memories scored **55.5 vs 71.0** (RegAgent), **13.1 vs 38.5** (EHRAgent) and **32.3 vs 51.0** (AgentDriver) against strict ground-truth-checked addition. Add-all fell *below* a fixed-memory baseline on every agent, by 3.7–12.1 points ([Xiong et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16067v2)). APC's looser matching cost 8.5 accuracy points ([APC](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.14852v2)). Full-trajectory injection underperforms structured reuse by 10.7 points in QCR ([QCR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12847)). Databricks reports agents reusing results from earlier *wrong* notebooks with more confidence, and querying renamed or deleted tables ([Databricks](https://www.databricks.com/blog/memory-scaling-ai-agents)). Outdated retrieved documents flip 30–37% of previously correct answers ([Stale-Document Poisoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31342)).

The counterweight is that success-filtered self-generated trajectories *do* help: ALFWorld rises from 73% to 89% when only successful trajectories accumulate ([Sarukkai et al., NeurIPS 2025](https://proceedings.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/5d1f02132ef51602adf07000ca5b6138-Abstract-Conference.html)). Ragtrail's hash validation addresses staleness. Its positive-outcome admission addresses error propagation only as well as the outcome signal is accurate. Paraphrased repeats are, by construction, the maximal-similarity case in which experience-following is strongest.

## Ragtrail's evaluation measures an upper bound, not a system

Ragtrail's measurements so far are −35% to −64% tool calls, −28% to −57% input tokens, and +11 to +16 judged accuracy points, on WixQA n=200 and QASPER n=400 with Haiku-paraphrased repeats and ORACLE trails. Prior evaluation practice flags four problems with this design.

**First, the workload is 100% repeat.** No cache paper reviewed paraphrases every test question. APC streams the benchmark from an empty cache and lets hits arise naturally, from 14% early to 48% overall ([APC](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.14852v2)). Real traffic repeats at roughly 30–40% in search logs ([Xie & O'Hallaron](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2002/06/infocom02.pdf)) and ~31% in ChatGPT conversations ([MeanCache](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.02694v3)). Ragtrail's numbers are therefore *hit-conditional*. Haiku paraphrases of Haiku-family inputs are also likely closer in surface form than real user rewordings, which inflates MinHash match rates.

**Second, oracle trails make the accuracy gain suspect.** A cache normally aims for parity; APC reports 96.6% of no-cache accuracy. A +11–16 point gain from trails pointing at gold evidence largely measures *gold-evidence leakage*.

**Third, the judge validation is too small to resolve the effect.** 32/40 agreement is 80%, with a 95% Wilson interval of roughly 65–90% (own calculation). That cannot exclude judge error of the same size as the effect. Percent agreement can hide score gaps of up to 20 points between judges ([Thakur et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.12624v2)), and same-family judges show self-preference ([Panickssery et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.13076v1)). Oracle-trail answers may also echo gold-article phrasing, so judge error could be *differential* by arm.

**Fourth, there is no variance accounting.** `claude -p` runs are stochastic. QASPER questions cluster by paper ([Miller](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00640v1)), and CLT intervals are too narrow at a few hundred items ([Bowyer et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.01747)).

### Concrete fixes to the evaluation methodology

**Workload and baselines**

- **Replace the all-repeat stream with a cold-start stream.** Start from an empty cache and use a natural or controlled repeat mix, for example 30% repeats. Report the hit-rate curve, hit-conditional savings, and system-level savings (saving × hit rate − miss/false-hit overhead).
- **Use human-written rather than Haiku-written repeats.** On WixQA, warm the cache on Simulated or Synthetic questions and test on ExpertWritten, which are independently written queries over the same knowledge base ([WixQA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.08643)). On QASPER, warm on some questions about a paper and test on *other* questions about the same paper. Add a vCache-Combo-style no-hit control set of questions that must not match, to measure false-hit damage ([vCache](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.03771v5)).
- **Turn the self-recorded follow-up into a four-arm design.** Mirror Xiong et al.'s add-all / strict / error-free comparison: no cache; self-recorded add-all; self-recorded admitted only on a deploy-time-available outcome signal; oracle. Report accuracy separately on repeats whose warm-up run was wrong. Add a deliberately corrupted-trail condition and a hint-vs-prefetch comparison, since prefetched text is the more likely carrier of experience-following.
- **Add a corpus-edit condition.** Mutate cited nodes and insert better new documents, as in GroundedCache's drift regime. Report the stale-serve rate for hash validation vs TTL-only, following the CIP and Alici comparisons, and separately the missed-new-evidence rate.

**Judging and statistics**

- **Strengthen judge validation.** Collect 100–200 human labels stratified by arm and report Cohen's kappa plus per-arm judge accuracy. Add a second judge from a different model family, use a correct / partial / incorrect rubric against the gold answer, and report answer length per arm.
- **Use paired statistics.** Run McNemar's exact test on discordant pairs. Report paired bootstrap CIs for accuracy deltas, clustered by QASPER paper and WixQA article. Use Wilcoxon or a paired bootstrap for tool and token reductions, report medians, and run multiple seeds per arm ([Dror et al.](https://aclanthology.org/P18-1128/)).

### The closest works to run against

Five comparisons would position ragtrail credibly.

- **APC** on QASPER. Both systems are filled from their own successful runs in the same streamed workload. Compare cost, tool calls and accuracy relative to no cache, plus APC's semantic-cache and full-history baselines ([APC](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.14852v2)).
- **A Proximity-style top-k id cache** on the agent's first search call. This tests whether multi-step path caching beats single-shot retrieval caching ([Proximity](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.05530v3)).
- **A GroundedCache-style gated answer cache.** This tests whether never caching the answer is worth its cost relative to gated answer reuse, under the same document-drift regime ([GroundedCache](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27494)).
- **A trajectory-as-exemplar memory**, Synapse/ExpeL-style full trajectories or a ReasoningBank-style distilled strategy injected as hint. This separates "reuse evidence ids" from "reuse search know-how" ([ReasoningBank](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.25140v2)).
- **QCR-style structured reuse**, if its code is available, as the strongest reuse-with-applicability-checks baseline ([QCR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12847)).

MERMAIDS caches cross-question evidence (as copied claim tuples, matched per sub-query); its ERC is a natural sixth baseline, specifically under a corpus-edit condition where its TTL-only freshness can be compared with ragtrail's per-node hash validation.

## Conclusion

The honest novelty claim is narrow and still defensible. Within the limits of this search (web search and abstracts through October 2026, no systematic SIGIR/CIKM/VLDB 2026 proceedings sweep, APEx/MIA/Hierarchical Caching read only at snippet level; MERMAIDS read in full), no work combines: a multi-step retrieval path with a cited-node subset and no answer; dual lexical MinHash plus embedding SimHash LSH in key-value buckets without a vector DB; per-node content-hash validation with *partial* downgrade; and permission-group scoping. Each part has an ancestor:

- id-not-answer caching and SimHash buckets in Proximity;
- hit-time freshness checks in Alici and GroundedCache;
- outcome-gated admission in APC, AWM and ReasoningBank;
- tenant partitioning in LangCache, CacheAttack and KVGov;
- past-query evidence reuse in Raghavan & Sever and Fitzpatrick & Dent.

The paper should claim the combination, the partial-downgrade validation, and the empirical fixed-context false-match result. It should not claim the parts.

The bigger risk is not priority but measurement. The literature predicts that ragtrail's accuracy gain will shrink, or turn negative on questions whose first run was wrong, once oracle trails give way to self-recorded ones. The savings will also shrink by the realistic hit rate once repeats stop being 100%. If ragtrail holds near-parity accuracy with self-recorded, outcome-filtered trails under realistic repeat rates and corpus edits, it lands squarely in the serving-cache tradition alongside APC, with a stronger freshness story than any of its neighbours. That result, not the oracle upper bound, is the one to lead with.
