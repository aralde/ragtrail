# Memory / experience for search and deep-research agents, and classic IR precedents for reusing past queries (prior art for ragtrail)

## Q1. Which agentic search / deep-research / RAG agents reuse memory of past searches, past evidence or past query→document mappings? What do they store, how do they match, do they validate freshness, what do they report?

### Takeaway
Two families exist, and neither is the same as ragtrail. (1) **Trajectory→retriever training** (LRAT, ITER) mines agent search/browse logs *offline* to train a better dense retriever; nothing question-specific is replayed at inference. (2) **Experience/strategy memory** (Databricks MemAlign, MIA, ReasoningBank, AWM, ReMe, R²-Mem, APEx, Memento) stores distilled workflows, strategies or episodic trajectories and injects them as in-context guidance. Explicit **question→cited-evidence replay with content-hash freshness validation** — ragtrail's core — was not found in any surveyed work; the closest mechanistic analogue is **approximate RAG caches** (Proximity) that reuse retrieved documents for semantically similar queries, which is retrieval-level, single-shot and without freshness validation.

### Cited Findings

**A. Trajectory-mined retrieval supervision (offline, no inference-time memory)**
- **LRAT — "Learning to Retrieve from Agent Trajectories"** (Zhou, Dai, Qu, Pang, Xu, Wen; arXiv 2604.04949, submitted 30 Mar 2026; **preprint**) [V]. Argues retrievers for agentic search should be trained on agent interaction data rather than human click signals. Signals: documents *browsed* after a search = positives; retrieved-but-unbrowsed = negatives; post-browse reasoning filtered by LLM judge, reasoning length used as a "relevance intensity" weight in weighted contrastive loss. Data: Tongyi-DeepResearch-30B on 10K InfoSeekQA queries, 26,482 trajectories, 91,713 training pairs. Citations are not used as a signal. Trajectories are used only to train a standard dense retriever; no trajectory reuse at inference — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.04949v1)
  - Reported: BrowseComp-Plus evidence-recall gains 16.8%–37.9% (e.g., Tongyi-DeepResearch 49.2→60.7; AgentCPM-Explore 23.2→32.0); success rate InfoSeek-Eval AgentCPM-Explore 40.3→55.7, MiniMax-M2.1 58.7→78.3; BrowseComp-Plus Tongyi-DeepResearch 17.8→23.7, GLM-4.7 43.9→54.6; average steps reduced "up to ~30%" (GLM-4.7 27.5→18.5 on InfoSeek-Eval) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.04949v1)
- **ITER — "Interaction-Aware Retrieval for Agentic Search"** (Chen, Wang, Yin, Zhuang, Zuccon, Leelanupab; arXiv 2608.27912, v1 28 Aug 2026, v2 10 Sep 2026; **preprint**) [V, abstract only]. Dense retriever that encodes main question + agent's pre-search reasoning + preceding sub-queries together with the current sub-query; trained from trajectory-derived signals (no gold labels). Over six agent backbones from three families, average relative gains over LRAT of 6.9% (InfoSeek-Eval) and 15.4% (BrowseComp-Plus); at 4B beats AgentIR on InfoSeek-Eval for 5/6 backbones. Ablation: previously visited-and-useful documents used as "redundancy negatives" give the strongest supervision — i.e., ITER explicitly learns to *avoid re-retrieving* already-seen evidence within a session — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27912)
- **"Agentic Search in the Wild: Intents and Trajectory Dynamics from 14M+ Real Search Requests"** (Ning, Coelho, Kong, Long, Martins, Magalhães, Callan, Xiong; arXiv 2601.17617, Jan 2026; v2 PDF lists **SIGIR '26** — treat as peer-reviewed if the SIGIR acceptance is confirmed) [B]. Log study of DeepResearchGym search API: 14.44M requests in 3.97M sessions; >90% of multi-turn sessions have ≤10 steps; 89% of inter-step gaps <1 minute; fact-seeking sessions show *increasing repetition* over time, reasoning sessions explore more broadly; proposes CTAR (Context-driven Term Adoption Rate) to trace new query terms to earlier retrieved evidence; recommends early stopping on repeated queries, intent-adaptive retrieval budgets, explicit cross-step context tracking; anonymized logs released on Hugging Face — [arXiv abs v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17617v2), [PDF v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.17617v2)

**B. Experience / memory reused at inference**
- **Databricks "Memory Scaling for AI Agents"** (Zhan, Lyu, Liu, Bendersky, Zaharia, Chen; 10 Apr 2026; **blog**) [V]. Stores episodic memory (conversation logs, tool-call trajectories, user feedback) and semantic memory (LLM-distilled rules/facts), personal and organizational scope; MemAlign retrieves relevant entries at inference; organizational knowledge store queried by keyword search or hierarchical browsing. Reported (no variance/sample sizes): on 10 Genie spaces with labeled data, score near zero→70% (~5 pts above expert-curated baseline), reasoning steps ~20→~5; on a live Genie space with unlabeled logs, 2.5%→>50% vs expert baseline 33.0% (surpassed after 62 log records), steps ~19→~4.3; org knowledge store ~10% accuracy gain on an internal benchmark and PMBench. Freshness/conflict handling (dedup, pruning outdated entries, provenance, re-validation) is stated as a *requirement*, not evaluated; ingestion filtered by an LLM judge — [Databricks blog](https://www.databricks.com/blog/memory-scaling-ai-agents)
- **MIA — Memory Intelligence Agent** (arXiv 2604.04503, 2026; **preprint**) [B, via search snippet]. Deep-research agent with a non-parametric Memory Manager storing *compressed historical search trajectories* as structured workflows retrieved as in-context examples for "search path planning and strategy reuse"; exploration trajectories also update the planner's parameters — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.04503)
- **APEx — Distillation of Agent Procedural Experience for Adaptive Deep Research QA** (arXiv 2609.02253, 2026; **preprint**) [B, snippet]. Builds instance-level memories and category-level skills, distilled into a Distiller–Planner–Executor setup — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.02253)
- **R²-Mem — Reflective Experience for Memory Search** (arXiv 2605.13486, 2026; **preprint**) [B, snippet]. Argues deep-search agents "treat each search trajectory in isolation"; distills process-level reflective experience from high- and low-quality trajectories — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13486)
- **ReMe — "Remember Me, Refine Me"** (arXiv 2512.10696, Dec 2025; **preprint**) [B, snippet]. Experience pool distilled from successful and failed trajectories; tracks utility of each experience across reuses (a usage-based validation, not content freshness) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.10696v2)
- **ExpGraph** (arXiv 2605.30712) and **Experience Memory Graph** (arXiv 2607.13884) (2026; **preprints**) [B, snippet]: graph-structured experience with utility-aware ranking; correction knowledge from failed trajectories — [ExpGraph](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.30712v1), [EMG](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.13884)
- **"Retrieval-Augmented LLM Agents: Learning to Learn from Experience"** (arXiv 2603.18272, 2026; **preprint**) [B, title only] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.18272v1)
- **ReasoningBank** (arXiv 2509.25140, 2025; **preprint**) [K]: abstracts successful and failed experiences into generalizable reasoning strategies (web-browsing/SWE agents) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25140)
- **Agent Workflow Memory (AWM)** (Wang et al., arXiv 2409.07429; ICML 2025 per later citations — verify) [K]: induces reusable workflows from successful web-navigation trajectories — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.07429)
- **ExpeL: LLM Agents Are Experiential Learners** (Zhao et al., AAAI 2024; **peer-reviewed**) [K]: stores successful trajectories + extracted insights, retrieves similar past trajectories as few-shot exemplars by task similarity — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.10144)
- **Memento: Fine-tuning LLM Agents without Fine-tuning LLMs** (arXiv 2508.16153, 2025; **preprint**) [K]: case-based memory of past deep-research trajectories (case bank) retrieved for planning — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.16153)

**C. Memory-augmented RAG (memory of the corpus, not of past questions)**
- **MemoRAG** (Qian et al., arXiv 2409.05591; later WWW 2025 per citations — verify) [K]: a light "global memory" model over the whole corpus generates draft clues that guide retrieval; memory is of the *corpus*, not of past queries — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.05591)
- **HippoRAG** (Gutiérrez et al., NeurIPS 2024; **peer-reviewed**) [K] and **HippoRAG 2** (arXiv 2502.14802) [K]: knowledge-graph "long-term memory" built at indexing time with Personalized PageRank retrieval; again corpus memory, not query→evidence memory — [HippoRAG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14831), [HippoRAG 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14802)
- **Search-o1** (arXiv 2501.05366) and **Search-R1** (arXiv 2503.09516) [K]: agentic search via prompting / RL; within-episode "reason-in-documents" condensation, no cross-question memory of searches — [Search-o1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.05366), [Search-R1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.09516)

**D. Cross-query retrieval caches for RAG (closest mechanism to ragtrail's replay)**
- **Proximity — "Leveraging Approximate Caching for Faster Retrieval-Augmented Generation"** (Bergman et al., arXiv 2503.05530; accepted at **Middleware '25**, peer-reviewed) [B]. Cache in front of the vector DB keyed on query embeddings with a similarity threshold; on hit, reuses previously retrieved documents. v3 adds Proximity-LSH; on a skewed MedRAG workload reduces DB calls by 77.2% "while maintaining database recall and test accuracy"; cache lookup ~4.8 µs. Evaluated on MMLU and MedRAG. No freshness/content validation described; single-shot RAG, not multi-step agent — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.05530)

### Inferences
- ragtrail's distinguishing combination appears novel relative to the surveyed set: (i) it stores *question→(searches, opened sections, cited sections)* per question rather than distilled strategies; (ii) it replays the *current text* of cited sections (prefetch) or their ids (hint); (iii) it validates by content hash. LRAT/ITER learn generic retrieval from the same kind of signals (browse/visit) but in weights, so they generalize but cannot short-circuit a specific repeat question; experience memories reuse *how* to search, not *what* was found.
- LRAT's "browsed = positive, unbrowsed = negative" and ITER's "visited useful docs as redundancy negatives" validate ragtrail's choice of opened/cited sections as the stored signal; ragtrail's use of *citations* is a stronger signal than browsing that LRAT does not use.
- Agentic Search in the Wild's finding of growing repetition in fact-seeking sessions and its recommendation of early stopping on repeated queries is direct empirical motivation for a trajectory cache (repetition exists in real agent traffic), though it measures within-session, not cross-user repetition.
- Databricks' step reductions (~20→~5) are of the same flavour as ragtrail's 35–64% fewer tool calls, but on text-to-SQL with distilled memory and no reported variance, so not directly comparable.
- Proximity is the closest "retrieval-result cache" baseline ragtrail should be positioned against: ragtrail differs by caching multi-step agent paths and citations, and by hash-validated freshness.

### Gaps
- Full texts of MIA, APEx, R²-Mem, ReMe, ExpGraph were not read; their exact storage format, matching, freshness handling and numbers are unverified.
- ITER full method/tables not read (PDF unparseable in this session); only abstract-level claims recorded.
- No surveyed paper reported content-hash or version-based freshness validation of memory entries; I could not confirm absence exhaustively.
- I did not find a paper that replays *cited evidence* for paraphrased repeat questions in an agentic loop and reports tool-call/token savings; if one exists after Aug 2026 it was not surfaced by search.
- Venue status of AWM (ICML 2025) and MemoRAG (WWW 2025) is from memory, not verified.

## Q2. Classic IR: which query-log / past-query / collaborative-search / caching / question-matching work are conceptual ancestors of reusing a question→evidence mapping?

### Takeaway
The direct ancestors are **Raghavan & Sever (SIGIR 1995)** — store "past optimal queries" and either reformulate a new query from them or *present their results* — and **Fitzpatrick & Dent (SIGIR 1997)** — use documents identified from similar past queries as feedback. Collaborative search (I-SPY) adds community click memory; result caching (Fagni et al.) adds the efficiency framing and the static/dynamic cache with prefetching; CQA question-to-question retrieval (Jeon/Croft/Lee) supplies the paraphrase-matching problem; re-finding (Teevan et al.) supplies the empirical case that repeats with different wording lead to the same results.

### Cited Findings
- **Raghavan & Sever, "On the Reuse of Past Optimal Queries," SIGIR 1995, pp. 344–350, DOI 10.1145/215206.215381 (peer-reviewed)** [B]. Defines similarity measures to retrieve past optimal queries, used either to reformulate the new query or to propose the past query's results; motivation: optimal queries are expensive to obtain so they should be saved and reused — [IR Anthology](https://ir.webis.de/anthology/1995.sigirconf_conference-95.41); description via [Hubert & Mothe 2007, "Reusing past queries to facilitate information retrieval"](https://www.scitepress.org/Papers/2007/13404/)
- **Hubert & Mothe, "Reusing Past Queries to Facilitate Information Retrieval," 2007 (SciTePress conference; peer-reviewed)** [B]: follow-up on past-query reuse — [SciTePress](https://www.scitepress.org/Papers/2007/13404/)
- **Fitzpatrick & Dent, "Automatic Feedback Using Past Queries: Social Searching?" SIGIR 1997, pp. 306–313, DOI 10.1145/258525.258597 (peer-reviewed)** [B]. In TREC, compared automatic feedback using documents from *similar past queries* vs standard top-document pseudo-feedback vs none; introduced a query-similarity metric based on comparing result lists with probability of relevance — [IR Anthology](https://ir.webis.de/anthology/1997.sigirconf_conference-97.34), [PDF mirror](https://static.aminer.org/pdf/20170130/pdfs/sigir/pfzoap7hlvsgdbvc0suu2k3emdtyj9t8.pdf)
- **Smyth et al., "Exploiting Query Repetition and Regularity in an Adaptive Community-Based Web Search Engine" (I-SPY), UMUAI 14(5):383–423, 2004 (peer-reviewed)** [B]. Meta-search front-end that records a community's query→selected-result hits and re-ranks results for similar queries; anonymous community personalization; a later live-user trial reported improved search performance vs a benchmark engine — [DOI 10.1007/s11257-004-5270-4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11257-004-5270-4), [Springer chapter on I-SPY](https://rd.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-0-85729-412-8_27)
- **Fagni, Perego, Silvestri, Orlando, "Boosting the Performance of Web Search Engines: Caching and Prefetching Query Results by Exploiting Historical Usage Data," ACM TOIS 24(1):51–78, 2006 (peer-reviewed)** [B]. Static-Dynamic Cache (SDC): static part filled from historical log's most frequent queries, dynamic part LRU-like, plus adaptive prefetching of following result pages; SDC beats purely static or dynamic policies on hit ratio over three large query logs — [PDF](https://www.dsi.unive.it/~orlando/PAPERS/TOIS.pdf), [dblp](https://dblp.uni-trier.de/rec/journals/tois/FagniPSO06.html)
- **Teevan, Adar, Jones, Potts, "Information Re-Retrieval: Repeat Queries in Yahoo's Logs," SIGIR 2007, pp. 151–158, DOI 10.1145/1277741.1277770 (peer-reviewed)** [B]. Up to 40% of queries are re-finding queries; a repeat search may use *different wording* yet lead to clicks on the same results; result changes hinder re-finding; proposes detecting repeat searches and predicting repeat clicks — [PDF](https://people.csail.mit.edu/teevan/work/publications/papers/sigir07.pdf), [MSR page](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/information-re-retrieval-repeat-queries-yahoos-logs/)
- **Jeon, Croft, Lee, "Finding Similar Questions in Large Question and Answer Archives," CIKM 2005, pp. 84–90, DOI 10.1145/1099554.1099572 (peer-reviewed)** [B]; companion SIGIR 2005 poster "Finding semantically similar questions based on their answers" computes question–question similarity using the *answers* too, catching paraphrases with little word overlap — [IR Anthology](https://ir.webis.de/anthology/2005.cikm_conference-2005.14)
- Further ancestors [K, not re-fetched]: Joachims, "Optimizing Search Engines using Clickthrough Data," KDD 2002 (click logs as relevance signal) — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/775047.775067); Beeferman & Berger, "Agglomerative Clustering of a Search Engine Query Log," KDD 2000 (query–URL click bipartite graph links queries sharing clicked documents) — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/347090.347176); Wen, Nie, Zhang, "Clustering User Queries of a Search Engine," WWW 2001 (queries clustered by shared clicked documents, for FAQ-like reuse) — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/371920.372094); Baeza-Yates et al., "The Impact of Caching on Search Engines," SIGIR 2007 — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/1277741.1277775); Burke et al., "Question Answering from Frequently Asked Question Files: Experiences with the FAQ Finder System," AI Magazine 1997 — [AAAI](https://ojs.aaai.org/aimagazine/index.php/aimagazine/article/view/1294); Morris & Horvitz, "SearchTogether," UIST 2007 (collaborative search sharing queries and found pages) — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/1294211.1294215); SemEval-2016 Task 3 CQA (question–question similarity subtask) — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/S16-1083/).

### Inferences
- ragtrail ≈ Raghavan & Sever's "propose the results of past optimal queries" + Fitzpatrick & Dent's "documents from similar past queries" + Fagni's cache/prefetch, transplanted to multi-step agents, with citations replacing relevance judgments/clicks as the success signal and content hashes replacing cache invalidation by TTL.
- Wen/Nie/Zhang and Beeferman & Berger define query similarity via *shared clicked documents*; ragtrail's stored "cited sections" give the same bipartite structure (question↔cited section) and could be used the same way (e.g., to cluster paraphrases or detect conflicting trails).
- Teevan et al.'s finding that result-list changes hinder re-finding is the classic statement of the freshness problem that ragtrail's hash validation addresses (re-fetch current text by id, not cached text).
- CQA duplicate-question detection is the matching sub-problem ragtrail faces with paraphrased repeats; Jeon et al.'s use of answers to match questions parallels matching on cited evidence.

### Gaps
- Abstracts/full texts of Raghavan & Sever and Jeon et al. were not retrieved; descriptions come from citing works and catalog records.
- Quantitative results of the classic papers (e.g., Fitzpatrick & Dent improvements, I-SPY trial numbers, SDC hit ratios) were not retrieved; do not quote numbers.
- [K]-marked items were not fetched in this session.

## Q3. Evidence that reused memories / past evidence hurt accuracy (stale, wrong, self-reinforcing)

### Takeaway
Yes, consistently: memory reuse can propagate errors ("experience-following"), wrong memories get reused with higher confidence, and stale memories point at renamed/deleted resources. Quality-gated insertion and deletion mitigate it. Poisoning attacks on agent memory/RAG stores are a further, adversarial version of the same risk.

### Cited Findings
- **Xiong et al., "How Memory Management Impacts LLM Agents: An Empirical Study of Experience-Following Behavior"** (arXiv 2505.16067, May 2025, v2 Oct 2025; **preprint**) [B]. High similarity between a task input and a retrieved memory's input yields highly similar outputs ("experience-following"); two failure modes: *error propagation* (inaccuracies in past experiences compound and degrade future performance) and *misaligned experience replay* (correct-looking records that are poor demonstrations). Erroneous records get added even with human inspection; every addition strategy showed an immediate gap vs. an error-free (ground-truth trajectory) counterpart. Mitigation: selective addition with evaluator feedback plus deletion; reported ~10% average absolute gain across three agents and three tasks — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.16067)
- **Databricks "Memory Scaling for AI Agents"** (blog, Apr 2026) [V]: "More memory does not automatically make an agent better"; low-quality traces teach wrong lessons; agents cited earlier notebooks that were wrong and reused those results *with more confidence* (self-reinforcement); staleness — an agent kept querying renamed or deleted tables; useful memories go unused if the agent does not think to query memory; distilled memory can leak sensitive patterns — [Databricks blog](https://www.databricks.com/blog/memory-scaling-ai-agents)
- **ReMe** tracks per-experience utility across reuses to demote unhelpful entries (implies non-trivial rate of unhelpful reuse) [B] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.10696v2)
- **R²-Mem** argues low-quality trajectories must be reflected on rather than stored raw [B] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13486)
- Adversarial [K]: **AgentPoison** (Chen et al., NeurIPS 2024) poisons agent long-term memory/RAG knowledge bases with triggered entries — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.12784); **PoisonedRAG** (Zou et al., USENIX Security 2025) injects corrupting passages into RAG corpora — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07867).
- Classic IR analogue: Teevan et al. show result changes between visits hinder re-finding (staleness of remembered result sets) [B] — [PDF](https://people.csail.mit.edu/teevan/work/publications/papers/sigir07.pdf)

### Inferences
- ragtrail's design addresses two of these failure modes directly — staleness (content hash; replay current text) — but not error propagation: a trail recorded from a wrong answer would steer future answers to the same sections. Xiong et al. and Databricks suggest the mitigation is quality-gated write (judge/evaluator or user feedback) plus deletion/demotion on failure.
- ragtrail's reported gains use *oracle warm-up trails*; the literature above implies gains will shrink, and could turn negative, when trails come from the agent's own (sometimes wrong) runs. An ablation with self-generated trails and a deliberately corrupted-trail condition would directly answer the "self-reinforcing memory" critique.
- Hint mode (ids only) vs prefetch mode may differ in error-propagation risk, since prefetched text is more likely to be "experience-followed" (Xiong et al.'s similarity→similar-output effect).
- Poisoning work implies a shared/multi-user trail store is an attack surface (a poisoned trail redirects later questions).

### Gaps
- No study found that quantifies harm specifically from replaying *cited evidence* (as opposed to strategies/trajectories) in RAG agents.
- No numbers found for how often stale memories occur in practice; Databricks reports anecdotes only.
- AgentPoison/PoisonedRAG details not re-verified this session.
