# Evaluation methodology for caches and memories in LLM agents and RAG (prior art for ragtrail)

Scope note: covers semantic/plan/RAG caches, agent memory, LLM-as-judge and paired statistics, up to Oct 2026. Venue status is given per source as [peer-reviewed: venue] or [preprint] / [blog/vendor]. All numbers below come from the cited source; where only a secondary summary was available it is flagged.

## 1. How cache papers build workloads with repeated / similar queries, and realistic repeat rates

### Takeaway
No cache paper reviewed builds its workload the way ragtrail does (LLM paraphrase of every test question = 100% near-duplicate rate). Published work either uses real logs (LM-Arena, ChatGPT conversations, search logs), human paraphrase pairs (Quora Question Pairs), datasets with a natural label/answer space where "same answer" defines a hit (vCache), or simply streams the benchmark and lets hits arise naturally (Agentic Plan Caching: 14-48% hit rate). Real traffic has roughly 20-40% repeated/similar queries, so a 100%-repeat workload measures the *per-hit* benefit, not the expected *system* benefit, and must be reported as such.

### Cited Findings
**Workload construction in cache papers**
- vCache (Schroeder et al.) [peer-reviewed: ICLR 2026] builds benchmarks from real datasets: SemBenchmarkClassification (45,000 short prompts from CommonsenseQA, e-commerce categorization, Amazon Instant Video reviews, fixed label space), SemBenchmarkLmArena (3,500 distinct prompts sampled from ~100k real LM-Arena user queries, each defining a class; responses from GPT-4.1-nano / GPT-4o-mini), SemBenchmarkCombo (27,500 prompts built so no two share the same expected response, i.e. a "no-hit" stress set), and a response-sorted variant. A hit is "correct" if the cached response matches the expected one; vCache sets a user error bound and maximizes hit rate under it, reporting up to 12.5x higher hit rate and 26x lower error rate than static-threshold baselines. — [arXiv 2502.03771v5](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.03771v5); [ICLR 2026 poster](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10006471); [SemBenchmarkLmArena card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/vCache/SemBenchmarkLmArena/blob/main/README.md); [SemBenchmarkClassification card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/vCache/SemBenchmarkClassification/blob/main/README.md)
- vCache argues static global similarity thresholds give no correctness guarantee and produce unexpected error rates — the key reason a cache eval must report false-hit/error rate, not only hit rate and savings. — [arXiv 2502.03771v5](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.03771v5)
- MeanCache (Gill et al.) [peer-reviewed: IPDPS 2025] analysed real ChatGPT conversations: ~31% of queries are similar to a previously submitted query (a later summary says ~33%). Its own test set *assumed* a 30% repeat fraction, justified by prior web-service studies (not measured on that set). Reports +17% F-score and ~20% precision over GPTCache for duplicate detection, +25% F-score on contextual queries. — [arXiv 2403.02694](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.02694v3); [UMN record](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/meancache-user-centric-semantic-caching-for-llm-web-services/)
- Agentic Plan Caching (APC; Zhang, Wornow, Wan, Olukotun) [peer-reviewed: NeurIPS 2025] evaluates on FinanceBench, **QASPER**, TabMWP, AIME 2024/2025 and GAIA. The cache starts empty and is filled by the system's own completed runs while streaming the benchmark (no paraphrase duplication reported). Hit rate rises from 14.29% (first 20% of queries) to 48.00% (all queries); 100 random FinanceBench queries give 46% hit rate. Averages: −50.31% cost, −27.28% latency, 96.61% of the accuracy of an always-GPT-4o planner. GAIA (Open Deep Research): cost $69.02 → $16.27, accuracy 37.58% → 36.97%. Fuzzy matching ablation: >80% similarity → 54% hit, 83.00% acc; >60% → 64% hit, 77.00% acc (looser matching costs accuracy). Judge: GPT-4o; no error bars or significance tests reported. — [arXiv 2506.14852v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.14852v2); [NeurIPS 2025 poster](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/poster/116166)
- APC baselines worth copying: query-level semantic cache at 80/85/90% thresholds and "full-history" (reuse whole execution logs as in-context examples). On FinanceBench full-history got 72.00% vs APC 85.50% accuracy. — [arXiv 2506.14852v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.14852v2)
- Proximity (Bergman et al.) [peer-reviewed: Middleware '25] — approximate cache of retrieved documents for RAG, evaluated on MMLU and MedRAG: up to 59% (MMLU) and 70.8% (MedRAG) lower retrieval latency with no or marginal accuracy loss; v3 adds an LSH variant and a "realistically skewed MedRAG workload" (−77.2% DB calls, −72.5% retrieval latency). Exact construction of query variations not retrieved. — [arXiv 2503.05530v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.05530v3)
- Quora Question Pairs is used as a cache benchmark because each pair is two independently written user questions; e.g. a 2025 ensemble-embedding cache paper [preprint] evaluates hit ratio and accuracy on QQP. Numbers not retrieved. — [arXiv 2507.07061](https://web3.arxiv.org/pdf/2507.07061)
- Category-aware semantic caching [preprint] reports, from a production system, that head categories hit often while tail categories hit rarely but still carry substantial traffic (numbers not retrieved). — [arXiv 2510.26835](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.26835)

**Realistic repeat rates**
- Search engines: Xie & O'Hallaron (Microsoft Research traces) [peer-reviewed: IEEE INFOCOM 2002] — ~30-40% of queries are repeats of earlier queries; ~16-22% are repeats by the same user. — [INFOCOM 2002 PDF](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2002/06/infocom02.pdf)
- Markatos (Excite log, ~1M queries) [peer-reviewed workshop: WCW 2000] — a significant fraction of queries are submitted more than once; later work summarizes this as 20-30%; the top 25 queries are only 1.23% of submissions; 75% is cited only as a theoretical hit-rate ceiling. — [Markatos 2000](https://projects.ics.forth.gr/dcs/Activities/papers/2000.WCW.caching_search.pdf)
- Chatbots: ~31% similar-to-previous in ChatGPT conversations (MeanCache, above).
- Practitioner reports [blog/vendor, not peer-reviewed]: one team's 100k production queries had 18% exact duplicates, semantic cache reached 67% hit rate, −73% API cost, 0.8% false positives. — [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/orchestration/why-your-llm-bill-is-exploding-and-how-semantic-caching-can-cut-it-by-73). Vendor threshold table for customer support (illustrative estimates): 0.93 cosine → 25-40% hits / 3-7% false positives; 0.85 → 45-70% hits / 15-30% false positives. — [Respan](https://respan.ai/articles/semantic-cache-llm)
- I found no peer-reviewed measurement of repeat rates for *customer-support desks* specifically (relevant to WixQA).

### Inferences
- ragtrail's paraphrase-every-question design is a legitimate *controlled* "hit-path" experiment, but its headline savings (−35 to −64% tool calls) are conditional on a hit. A defensible system-level number = hit-conditional saving × realistic hit rate (e.g. 20-40%), plus the cost of misses/false hits. Report both.
- Paraphrases generated by Haiku (same family as the agent) are likely closer in surface form than real user rephrasings (QQP / LM-Arena style), inflating match rates. Mitigations: use human paraphrase sets (QQP-style), WixQA ExpertWritten vs Simulated (two independently written question sets on the same KB), or mtRAG's multi-turn rewrites; add a "no-hit" control set (vCache Combo style) of new questions that *should not* match, to measure false-hit damage.
- APC is the closest prior art and also used QASPER; its methodology (empty cold-start cache, natural hit rate curve, semantic-cache and full-history baselines, accuracy relative to no-cache) is the template reviewers will expect.

### Gaps
- Exact paraphrase/variation procedure in Proximity and the QQP numbers in 2507.07061 were not retrieved.
- No peer-reviewed repeat-rate study for enterprise support/help-center traffic found.

## 2. Oracle vs self-generated memories / trajectories

### Takeaway
Gains from memory measured with oracle (ground-truth) memories are an upper bound. When memories are the agent's own outputs added indiscriminately, the strongest controlled study finds performance falls *below having no new memory at all*, because agents copy retrieved trajectories ("experience-following") and propagate errors. Self-generated memories do help when they are filtered by a success signal (evaluator/ground truth). ragtrail's oracle-trail result should therefore be reported as an upper bound, and the self-recorded follow-up needs a filter condition and an unfiltered condition.

### Cited Findings
- Xiong et al., "How Memory Management Impacts LLM Agents: An Empirical Study of Experience-Following Behavior" [preprint, arXiv v2 Oct 2025]: high similarity between task input and a retrieved record's input yields highly similar outputs (RegAgent: r≈1 at high similarity). Flawed retrieved records are replicated and amplified ("error propagation"), and flawed outputs re-enter memory. — [arXiv 2505.16067v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16067v2)
- Same paper, final performance (GPT-4o-mini backbone): add-all vs strict (ground-truth-checked) addition — RegAgent 55.48 vs 70.95; EHRAgent 13.05 vs 38.50; AgentDriver 32.32 vs 51.00; CIC-IoT 59.90 vs 85.40. Add-all is *below* a fixed-memory baseline on every agent (by 12.05, 3.70, 7.79, 11.60 points). — [arXiv 2505.16067v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16067v2)
- Same paper compares against an "error-free" baseline where retrieved executions are replaced by ground truth: an immediate gap appears and add-all/coarse-evaluator addition widen it over time (gap shown only in a figure). With strict addition plus deletion, AgentDriver eventually surpasses the error-free baseline. History-based deletion also shrinks memory (e.g. EHRAgent 1012 → 248 records) while improving accuracy. — [arXiv 2505.16067v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16067v2)
- Sarukkai, Xie, Fatahalian [peer-reviewed: NeurIPS 2025]: accumulating only *successful* self-generated trajectories as in-context examples raises ALFWorld 73% → 89%, Wordcraft 55% → 64%, InterCode-SQL 75% → 79%; with database- and exemplar-level curation ALFWorld reaches 93% (HF summary says 91%; version discrepancy). Success filtering uses the environment's reward. — [NeurIPS 2025 proceedings](https://proceedings.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/5d1f02132ef51602adf07000ca5b6138-Abstract-Conference.html); [arXiv 2505.00234](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.00234v3)
- Agent Workflow Memory (Wang, Mao, Fried, Neubig) [peer-reviewed: ICML 2025] distinguishes offline induction (from annotated examples) and online, supervision-free induction from self-generated trials judged correct by an evaluator module; reported +24.6% and +51.1% relative success on Mind2Web and WebArena (details from secondary summaries). — [ICML 2025 poster](https://icml.cc/virtual/2025/poster/45496); [arXiv 2409.07429](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.07429)
- APC builds its cache from its own completed runs (not oracle) and filters context-specific details with rules + GPT-4o-mini; it still reaches 96.61% of the no-cache planner's accuracy, i.e. reported as *near-parity*, not as an accuracy gain. — [arXiv 2506.14852v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.14852v2)

### Inferences
- ragtrail's +11 to +16 point accuracy gain is suspicious in a cache paper: caches normally aim for parity at lower cost (APC: 96.61% of optimal). With oracle trails citing gold articles/evidence, the "gain" largely measures *answer leakage from gold evidence*, not caching. Frame oracle results as an upper bound ("perfect trail" condition) and lead with the self-recorded result.
- The self-recorded follow-up should include at least: (a) add-all self trails, (b) trails filtered by a success signal available at deploy time (judge or user feedback), (c) oracle trails, and (d) no-cache — mirroring the add-all / strict / error-free design of Xiong et al. Report accuracy on questions whose warm-up run was *wrong*: that is where error propagation shows.
- Expect experience-following: paraphrased repeats are maximally similar inputs, so the agent will likely copy the trail's answer, right or wrong. Accuracy on hits ≈ accuracy of the original trail.

### Gaps
- No study found specifically on trajectory caches for agentic RAG comparing oracle vs self-recorded trails; the closest are general agent-memory papers above.

## 3. LLM-as-judge best practice and paired statistics

### Takeaway
LLM judges reach roughly human-level *percent* agreement on easy settings, but percent agreement hides large score differences; report chance-corrected agreement (Cohen's kappa / Scott's pi) and the judge-vs-human *accuracy difference per arm*. Known biases (position, verbosity, self-preference, leniency) matter for ragtrail because the judge (Sonnet) is the same family as the agent (Haiku) and arms may differ in answer length. 40 validation labels give a wide interval; paired per-question tests with small-sample-valid intervals are expected.

### Cited Findings
- Zheng et al. (MT-Bench / Chatbot Arena) [peer-reviewed: NeurIPS 2023 Datasets & Benchmarks]: GPT-4 judge reaches over 80% agreement with humans, the same level as human-human agreement; identifies position, verbosity and self-enhancement bias and limited reasoning (e.g. on math). — [arXiv 2306.05685](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685)
- Thakur et al., "Judging the Judges" [preprint; multiple versions]: on TriviaQA with 9 judges and 9 exam-takers, judges with 80%+ percent agreement can assign scores up to 20 points apart; Cohen's kappa is more informative (GPT-4 Turbo 84, Llama-3 70B 79, humans 96); even well-aligned judges can differ by up to 5 points from human scores. Judges tend to be lenient and can be fooled by answers like "Yes", "Sure", "I don't know". Later versions report Scott's pi instead of kappa. — [arXiv 2406.12624v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.12624v2); [v5](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.12624v5)
- Panickssery, Bowman, Feng [peer-reviewed: NeurIPS 2024 oral]: GPT-3.5, GPT-4 and Llama 2 favor their own summaries; self-preference strength correlates linearly with self-recognition ability (GPT-4 73.5% accurate at distinguishing its outputs from two other LLMs and humans). — [arXiv 2404.13076](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.13076v1); [NeurIPS 2024](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2024/oral/97998)
- Wang et al., "Large Language Models are not Fair Evaluators" [peer-reviewed: ACL 2024]: pairwise judge rankings can be flipped by swapping answer order; proposes multiple-evidence calibration and balanced position calibration. — [arXiv 2305.17926](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17926)
- APC (closest prior art) uses GPT-4o as judge for numeric and long-form answers, arguing LLM evaluation beats exact match/F1 there, but reports no error bars. — [arXiv 2506.14852v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.14852v2)
- Miller, "Adding Error Bars to Evals" [preprint, Anthropic, 2024]: report standard errors; use clustered SEs when questions share a source (e.g. several QASPER questions on one paper); for two systems on the same questions, analyse *paired* per-question differences (reduces variance); do power analysis to choose n; CLT is adequate for many questions, bootstrap unnecessary unless the estimator/sampling is complicated. — [arXiv 2411.00640](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00640v1)
- Bowyer, Aitchison, Ivanova [peer-reviewed: ICML 2025 position/spotlight]: CLT intervals are too narrow for benchmarks with fewer than a few hundred items; recommends alternative frequentist/Bayesian intervals (library bayes_evals); non-iid sub-tasks should be handled separately. — [arXiv 2503.01747](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.01747); [ICML 2025](https://icml.cc/virtual/2025/poster/40132)
- Card et al., "With Little Power Comes Great Responsibility" [peer-reviewed: EMNLP 2020]: underpowered comparisons are common in NLP; small test sets make most comparisons underpowered; recommends prior power analysis (notebooks released). — [arXiv 2010.06595](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.06595)
- Dror et al., "The Hitchhiker's Guide to Testing Statistical Significance in NLP" [peer-reviewed: ACL 2018]: guidance on choosing significance tests (paired/non-parametric tests such as McNemar, sign, bootstrap, permutation) by metric type. — [ACL Anthology P18-1128](https://aclanthology.org/P18-1128/)

### Inferences
- ragtrail's 32/40 = 80% exact agreement has a 95% Wilson interval of roughly 65%-90% (own calculation). That cannot rule out a judge error rate comparable to the reported +11-16 point effect. Needed: (a) ≥100-200 human labels stratified by arm (roughly half cache, half baseline), (b) kappa plus per-arm judge accuracy, and (c) a check that judge error is not *differential* between arms (e.g. cache answers that copy gold-article wording may be favoured).
- Same-family risk: Sonnet judging Haiku outputs fits the self-preference setting; it affects both arms equally only if both arms' answers are equally "Claude-like". Oracle trails inject gold-text phrasing into the cache arm, which could raise judge scores for stylistic reasons. Mitigate with a second judge from another family (e.g. GPT or Gemini) and report agreement between judges; score against the gold answer with a rubric (correct / partially / incorrect) rather than holistic quality; control/report answer length per arm (verbosity bias).
- Statistics for a paired binary outcome on n=200/400: McNemar exact test on discordant pairs, paired bootstrap CI of the accuracy difference (resample questions; cluster by QASPER paper / WixQA article), Wilcoxon or paired bootstrap for tool-call and token reductions; report medians as well as means. Run each arm with multiple seeds or repeated samples since claude -p is stochastic.

### Gaps
- Did not find a consensus "recommended validation sample size" for judge calibration; literature gives principles (kappa, power) rather than a fixed n.
- Position bias is less relevant here (single-answer grading against a reference), but no source was fetched quantifying single-answer reference-based judge bias for RAG specifically.

## 4. Benchmarks for agentic RAG / deep-research cost-quality evaluation and natural repeat structure

### Takeaway
Standard benchmarks (HotpotQA, FRAMES, BrowseComp, MultiHop-RAG, QASPER) are sets of distinct questions with no natural repeats; repeats must be simulated. Datasets with natural repeat/near-duplicate structure are: WixQA (ExpertWritten real customer queries + Simulated set on the same KB, and 6k synthetic one-per-article questions), QASPER (multiple questions per paper, often from several annotators), mtRAG (multi-turn conversations over shared corpora), and real-log sets (LM-Arena, ChatGPT logs, QQP).

### Cited Findings
- WixQA (Cohen et al., Wix) [preprint, May 2025]: ExpertWritten = 200 genuine customer queries with expert step-by-step answers; Simulated = 200 expert-validated QA pairs distilled from user dialogues; Synthetic = ~6,221 LLM-generated pairs, one per KB article; KB snapshot released (MIT). Answers can draw on multiple articles. Simulated is the hardest set, Synthetic the easiest; E5 beats BM25 in context recall (0.81 vs 0.73 on ExpertWritten). — [arXiv 2505.08643](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.08643)
- QASPER (Dasigi et al.) [peer-reviewed: NAACL 2021]: information-seeking questions over NLP papers with evidence annotations; used as a workload by APC (NeurIPS 2025). — [arXiv 2105.03011](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.03011); [APC](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.14852v2)
- mtRAG (Katsis, Rosenthal et al., IBM) [peer-reviewed: TACL]: 110 human-generated multi-turn conversations, avg 7.7 turns, 842 tasks, 4 corpora; plus 200 synthetic conversations (mtRAG-S). Follow-up MTRAG-UN [preprint, Feb 2026]: 666 tasks over 2,800 turns, 6 domains, unanswerable/underspecified questions; used in SemEval 2026 MTRAGEval. — [arXiv 2501.03468](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.03468v1); [GitHub](https://github.com/IBM/mt-rag-benchmark); [MTRAG-UN](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2602.23184)
- APC's agentic cost-quality suite: FinanceBench, QASPER, TabMWP, AIME 2024/2025, GAIA (with smolagents Open Deep Research). — [arXiv 2506.14852v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.14852v2)
- Other commonly used agentic-RAG / deep-research benchmarks (listed for reference; repeat structure is none by design): HotpotQA [peer-reviewed: EMNLP 2018] — [arXiv 1809.09600](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.09600); FRAMES [peer-reviewed: NAACL 2025] — [arXiv 2409.12941](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.12941); MultiHop-RAG [peer-reviewed: COLM 2024] — [arXiv 2401.15391](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.15391); BrowseComp [preprint, OpenAI 2025] — [arXiv 2504.12516](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.12516); MedRAG / MMLU used by Proximity — [arXiv 2503.05530v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.05530v3).

### Inferences
- For ragtrail, the most defensible "natural repeat" protocol on WixQA: warm the cache on one question set (e.g. Simulated or Synthetic questions about the same articles) and test on ExpertWritten, so repeats are topical overlaps written by different people, not Haiku paraphrases. On QASPER: warm on some questions about a paper, test on *other* questions about the same paper (shared-document reuse rather than duplicate question).
- Adding one APC-overlap benchmark (QASPER already overlaps) allows a direct comparison with the closest prior art; GAIA/FRAMES would show whether the cache helps where questions do not repeat (expected: hit rate ~0, overhead only).

### Gaps
- Venue details for FRAMES/MultiHop-RAG/QASPER and benchmark sizes were not re-verified in this session (links are to arXiv records).
- No benchmark found that ships real repeated-query traces for agentic RAG specifically.
